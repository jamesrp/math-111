Mean, median and standard deviation

1. Hours of sleep of 253 college students

Pamela Thacher, a psychologist at St. Lawrence University in New York State, and her co-author Serge Onyper studied the sleep of 253 college students [1]. Each student kept a sleep diary, took tests of thinking skills and answered a survey about habits and mood [2]. The data are public [2, 3]. The R package Lock5Data, which also contains these data, is licensed under GPL-2 [4]. Two of the columns are used here:

  • AverageSleep: the student's average number of hours of sleep per night, over weekdays and weekends together.
  • ClassesMissed: the number of classes the student missed during the semester.

2. The mean and summation notation

The number in Task 1(a) is the mean of the list. The mean of a list of numbers is their sum divided by how many numbers there are. It is what people usually call "the average".

To write this for any list, give the pieces names:

  • n is how many values there are. For the table, n = 10.
  • x1, x2, …, xn are the values. Here x1 = 7.18, x2 = 6.93, and so on up to x10 = 6.68.
  • x, read "x-bar", is the mean.

With the summation notation from lecture 4.2, the mean is

x = x1 + x2 + … + xnn = 1nnΣi = 1xi

The capital Greek letter Σ ("sigma") with i = 1 below it and n above it, followed by xi, means: put i = 1, 2, 3, …, n into xi and add the results. For the 10 students,

10Σi = 1xi = 73.17  and  x = 73.1710 = 7.317 hours.

The mean has the same unit as the data, here hours of sleep per night. It is the balance point of the list: the values above it and the values below it are, in total, the same distance from it.

3. The median, and when it is the better summary

The median is the middle value of a list sorted from smallest to largest. If n is odd, there is one middle value. If n is even, there are two, and the median is the mean of those two.

The 10 sleep values, sorted:

5.02, 6.35, 6.68, 6.90, 6.93, 7.18, 7.52, 8.54, 9.01, 9.04

The 5th and 6th values are 6.93 and 7.18, so the median is (6.93 + 7.18) ÷ 2 = 7.055, about 7.06 hours. Half of the 10 students (5 of them) sleep less than the median. In Task 1(c), 6 of the 10 sleep less than the mean, because the mean (7.32) is a little larger than the median (7.06). For these sleep values the two are close.

Task 2 (about 3 minutes, calculator)

The classes missed by the 10 students in the table are 0, 0, 12, 0, 4, 0, 2, 0, 0, 0.

  1. Find the mean and the median number of classes missed.
  2. Suppose student 3 had missed 0 classes instead of 12. Find the mean and the median again.
  3. Which of the two numbers from (a) describes a typical student among these 10 better? Write one sentence saying why.
Solution to Task 2

(a) Sum: 0 + 0 + 12 + 0 + 4 + 0 + 2 + 0 + 0 + 0 = 18. Mean: 18 ÷ 10 = 1.8 classes. Sorted: 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 2, 4, 12. The 5th and 6th values are both 0, so the median is 0 classes.

(b) Sum: 6. Mean: 6 ÷ 10 = 0.6 classes. Sorted: 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 2, 4. The median is still 0.

(c) The median. Seven of the 10 students missed no classes, and the mean of 1.8 is pulled up by student 3 alone: changing that one value moves the mean from 1.8 to 0.6 and leaves the median at 0.

A value far from the rest of the data, like student 3's 12 missed classes, is called an extreme value. The mean uses the size of every value, so one extreme value moves it. The median uses only the order of the values, so making the largest value even larger does not change the median at all.

Use the median when a few values are far out on one side, as with classes missed. Use the mean when the values are spread fairly evenly on both sides of the middle, as with the sleep values. When the two are close, either one describes the center.

For all 253 students in the study, the mean number of classes missed is 2.21 and the median is 1. Of the 253 students, 94 (37%) missed no classes.

4. Range and standard deviation

The mean and the median describe the center of the data. Two groups can have the same mean and still differ a lot: in one group everyone could sleep close to 8 hours, in another some could sleep 5 hours and some 11. A second number describes this spread.

The simplest is the range: the largest value minus the smallest. For the 10 students in Section 1 it is 9.04 − 5.02 = 4.02 hours. The range uses only the two most extreme values, so one unusual student can make it large.

Task 3 (about 3 minutes, calculator)

Two groups of five students from the data set average these hours of sleep:

  • Group A (students 61, 73, 120, 124 and 224): 7.96, 7.90, 8.00, 8.20, 7.94
  • Group B (students 25, 46, 102, 108 and 126): 8.0, 8.3, 6.5, 9.8, 7.4
  1. Find the mean and the range of each group.
  2. For group A, subtract the mean from each of the five values, then add the five differences. Write down the five differences and their sum.
Solution to Task 3

(a) Group A: (7.96 + 7.90 + 8.00 + 8.20 + 7.94) ÷ 5 = 40.00 ÷ 5 = 8.00 hours. Range: 8.20 − 7.90 = 0.30 hours.

Group B: (8.0 + 8.3 + 6.5 + 9.8 + 7.4) ÷ 5 = 40.0 ÷ 5 = 8.0 hours. Range: 9.8 − 6.5 = 3.3 hours.

(b) The differences are −0.04, −0.10, 0.00, 0.20 and −0.06. Their sum is 0.

Both groups have mean 8.0 hours, and group B is far more spread out: its range is 11 times as large. A natural way to measure spread is to ask how far the values typically are from the mean. Averaging the differences from the mean does not work. They always add up to 0, as in Task 3(b), because the mean is the balance point: the negative differences cancel the positive ones. The standard deviation removes the signs by squaring.

The standard deviation (SD) uses every value. It is roughly the typical distance of the values from their mean. For values x1, …, xn with mean x:

  1. Find the mean x.
  2. For each value, find its deviation from the mean: xi − x. Values below the mean have negative deviations.
  3. Square each deviation.
  4. Add the squares and divide the total by n − 1.
  5. Take the square root.

In one formula, the standard deviation s is

s = √nΣi = 1(xi − x)2n − 1

Notation. These symbols mean the same thing in this lecture and in lectures 8.2 and 8.3.

n: the number of values in the data set, also called the sample size. For the full study, n = 253.

x ("x-bar"): the mean of the values, also called the sample mean.

s: the standard deviation computed with the five steps above, dividing by n − 1. It is also called the sample standard deviation, and research papers write it as SD.

Why each step

Why square the deviations. The deviations always add up to 0, as in Task 3(b) and in the table above, so their average says nothing about spread. Squaring makes every deviation positive (or 0), so they no longer cancel. Squaring also counts large deviations more heavily than small ones.

Why the square root. The squares are in hours², a unit that is hard to picture. The square root brings the answer back to hours, the unit of the data.

Why divide by n − 1. Research papers and the spreadsheet function STDEV divide by n − 1, and this course does the same. The reason belongs to a statistics course. Dividing by n = 5 instead would give √(5.94 ÷ 5) = 1.09 hours for group B. For large data sets the choice hardly matters: for all 253 students the two versions give 0.965 and 0.963 hours.

Reading the answer. The distances of the five group B students from the mean are 0, 0.3, 1.5, 1.8 and 0.6 hours. An SD of 1.22 hours says that these students sleep roughly 1.2 hours more or less than the mean of 8.0. The plain average of the five distances is 0.84 hours. The SD comes out larger because squaring gives the larger distances more weight, which is why the SD is only roughly the typical distance.

6. Histograms

A histogram shows how the values of a data set are spread out. To draw one, split the number line into intervals of equal width, called bins, count how many values fall into each bin, and draw a bar over each bin whose height is that count. In Figure 1 the bins are half an hour wide: 4.5 to 5 hours, 5 to 5.5 hours, and so on. A value on the boundary between two bins goes into the bin on its right, so 7.0 hours counts in the bin from 7 to 7.5.

Task 6 (about 3 minutes, Figures 1 and 2)

  1. In Figure 1, which bin has the most students, and how many students are in it?
  2. From Figure 1, how many of the 253 students average less than 7 hours of sleep? Write this as a percentage of 253.
  3. From Figure 2, how many students missed 0 or 1 classes?
Solution to Task 6

(a) The bin from 7.5 to 8 hours, with 56 students. The bin from 8 to 8.5 hours is close behind with 55.

(b) Add the bars to the left of 7: 1 + 1 + 5 + 14 + 15 = 36 students. As a percentage: 36 ÷ 253 = 0.142, about 14%.

(c) 143 students, the first bar.

The two histograms have different shapes. Figure 1 has one peak in the middle, near 8 hours, and falls off on both sides at about the same rate. A histogram with this shape is called bell-shaped. Figure 2 has its tallest bar at the left and a long, thin tail to the right: a few students missed 10 or more classes. A histogram with a long tail on the right is called skewed to the right. In a right-skewed data set the values in the tail pull the mean up, so the mean is larger than the median, as in Section 5.

7. How much of the data lies within one or two standard deviations

The sleep data of all 253 students have mean x = 7.97 hours and SD s = 0.96 hours. "Within one SD of the mean" means between 7.97 − 0.96 and 7.97 + 0.96 hours. "Within two SDs" means between 7.97 − 2 × 0.96 and 7.97 + 2 × 0.96 hours. In Figure 3, the slider sets how many SDs to go out on each side of the mean, and the count below the figure gives the number of students in that interval.

Task 7 (about 3 minutes, Figure 3 and calculator)

  1. With a calculator, find the interval within one SD of the mean: from 7.97 − 0.96 to 7.97 + 0.96 hours. Then set the slider to 1, check that the dashed lines are at your two numbers, and write down the number of students inside. Turn that number into a percentage of 253.
  2. Do the same for two SDs, with the slider set to 2.
Solution to Task 7

(a) From 7.97 − 0.96 = 7.01 to 7.97 + 0.96 = 8.93 hours. 181 students, and 181 ÷ 253 = 0.715, about 72%.

Adding the four bars from 7 to 9 hours gives 34 + 56 + 55 + 39 = 184, three more than 181. The bars include two students at exactly 7.00 hours, below 7.01, and one at 8.94 hours, above 8.93. These three are outside the interval.

(b) From 7.97 − 1.92 = 6.05 to 7.97 + 1.92 = 9.89 hours. 240 students, and 240 ÷ 253 = 0.949, about 95%.

Many data sets with a bell-shaped histogram follow the same pattern, which gives a rule of thumb:

Rule of thumb. If a histogram is roughly bell-shaped, about 68% of the values (roughly two-thirds) lie within one SD of the mean, and about 95% lie within two SDs of the mean.

The sleep data come close: 72% within one SD and 95% within two. The percentages 68% and 95% come from an ideal bell-shaped curve, called the normal curve, which real data follow only roughly. That is why the rule says "about".

The rule does not apply to skewed data. For classes missed, the mean is 2.21 and the SD is 3.24. One SD below the mean is 2.21 − 3.24 = −1.03 classes, which is impossible, and 223 of the 253 students (88%) lie within one SD of the mean instead of about 68%. For data like these, the median and a histogram describe the students better than the mean and SD.

8. Reading a reported mean and standard deviation

When a paper reports only the mean and SD of a measurement, the rule of thumb turns those two numbers into a picture of the group, as long as the histogram is roughly bell-shaped, as it is for the sleep data in Figure 1.

Task 8 (about 3 minutes, calculator)

A study reports that the people in it sleep "mean 7.1 hours, SD 1.2 hours". These are the made-up numbers from Section 1. Assume the histogram is roughly bell-shaped.

  1. Between which two numbers of hours do about two-thirds of the people sleep?
  2. Between which two numbers of hours do about 95% of the people sleep?
  3. Is a person in this study who sleeps 4 hours a night unusual? Give a reason using (b).
Solution to Task 8

(a) Within one SD: 7.1 − 1.2 = 5.9 to 7.1 + 1.2 = 8.3 hours.

(b) Within two SDs: 7.1 − 2.4 = 4.7 to 7.1 + 2.4 = 9.5 hours.

(c) Yes. 4 hours is below 4.7, so this person is outside the interval that holds about 95% of the group. Only about 5% of people are outside it, and if the histogram is symmetric, about half of those, roughly 2.5%, are on the low side.

So "mean 7.1 hours, SD 1.2 hours" says that a typical person in the study sleeps about 7 hours, that about two-thirds of the group sleep within 1.2 hours of that, and that sleeping less than about 4.7 or more than about 9.5 hours is rare in the group. The two numbers do not tell you how much any particular person sleeps, and they do not show whether the data are skewed. When the mean and median of a measurement are far apart, or a histogram has a long tail, the median and the histogram tell you more than the mean and SD.
