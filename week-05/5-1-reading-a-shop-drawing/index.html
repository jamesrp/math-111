Math 111 · Module: Remodeling a kitchen (week 5) · Lecture 1 of 3

Reading a shop drawing

A kitchen remodel starts from a floor plan drawn to scale. Figure 1 is the plan of an L-shaped kitchen at the scale 1/4 inch = 1 foot. The plan is made up for this example. The lengths written on it are what a ruler read on the original printed plan, in inches and fractions of an inch, as on a tape measure.

The new floor will be Lifeproof Trail Oak vinyl plank. On October 4, 2026, homedepot.com listed it at $2.49 per square foot, sold in cases that cover 20.06 square feet for $49.95 a case [1].

The questions: how big is the kitchen floor really, how much flooring do you need to buy, and what will it cost?

Simplifications used throughout: walls are drawn as lines with no thickness, and the small expansion gap that the flooring leaves at walls and cabinets is ignored.

1. The scale as a rate

The scale 1/4 in = 1 ft says that every quarter inch on the paper stands for one foot of real floor. Four quarter inches make an inch, so one inch on the paper stands for 4 feet. Written as a rate, as in week 1:

4 ft ─────────── (4 feet of floor per inch of paper) 1 in on paper

On this page "in on paper" means an inch measured on the drawing, and "in" alone means a real inch of floor. So "1 in on paper = 4 ft = 48 in" does not contradict "1 ft = 12 in".

To change a paper length into a real length, multiply by this rate. The inches on paper cancel and feet are left. For the top wall, 3 5/8 in on paper:

3 5/8 in on paper = 3 + 5/8 = 3 + 0.625 = 3.625 in on paper 3.625 in on paper × (4 ft / 1 in on paper) = 14.5 ft

A tape measure reads feet and inches, so turn the decimal part of a foot into inches with the rate 12 in per ft:

14.5 ft = 14 ft + 0.5 ft × (12 in / 1 ft) = 14 ft 6 in

Fractions of an inch on this plan

Rulers and tape measures mark inches in halves, quarters, eighths and sixteenths. On this plan each small mark has a fixed real size:

So a length in sixteenths of an inch converts quickly: count the sixteenths and multiply by 3 real inches. For 1 5/16 in on paper, there are 16 sixteenths in the 1 and 5 more, so 21 sixteenths:

21 × 3 in = 63 in = 5 × 12 in + 3 in = 5 ft 3 in

The last row of the table gives the scale as a ratio. One foot is 12 inches, so 1/4 inch on paper stands for 12 inches of floor, and 12 ÷ (1/4) = 48. Every real length is 48 times its length on paper. As a ratio, this scale is 1 : 48.

2. Subtracting fractions of an inch and adding feet and inches

The bottom edge a and the right edge b in Figure 1 have no length given. Each is the difference of two lengths that are labeled.

The bottom edge a is the top wall minus the notch: 3 5/8 in − 1 5/16 in, both on paper. To subtract fractions, write them with the same denominator. Eighths become sixteenths by multiplying top and bottom by 2: 5/8 = 10/16.

3 10/16 in − 1 5/16 in = 2 5/16 in 2 5/16 in on paper = 37 sixteenths 37 × 3 in = 111 in = 9 ft 3 in (as a decimal: 2.3125 × 4 ft = 9.25 ft)

Sometimes the fraction you subtract is bigger than the fraction on top. Then borrow 1 inch from the whole number and write it as a fraction: 1 = 16/16, or 1 = 8/8. For example, 4 3/16 in − 1 5/16 in: 5/16 cannot come out of 3/16, so write 4 3/16 as 3 + 16/16 + 3/16 = 3 19/16.

3 19/16 in − 1 5/16 in = 2 14/16 in = 2 7/8 in

Check by adding in feet and inches. Add the inches, then the feet: 9 ft 3 in + 5 ft 3 in = 14 ft 6 in, which is the top wall.

When the inches add up to 12 or more, carry 12 inches as 1 foot, the way 10 ones become 1 ten in ordinary addition. For example, 5 ft 9 in + 2 ft 8 in = 7 ft 17 in = 7 ft + 12 in + 5 in = 8 ft 5 in.

  3. Check: add b and the notch height 4 ft 6 in in feet and inches. You should get the left wall, 12 ft.
Solution to Task 2

  3. 7 ft 6 in + 4 ft 6 in = 11 ft 12 in. Carry 12 in as 1 ft: 12 ft 0 in. This matches the left wall.

3. Area of the L-shaped floor

The area of a rectangle is length × width. If both are in feet, the area is in square feet (sq ft): the number of 1 ft by 1 ft squares that cover it.

The kitchen floor is not a rectangle, so a single length × width does not apply. Draw a line that cuts the L into two rectangles, find each area, and add. Figure 2 uses the real lengths from Tasks 1 and 2 and cuts along the dashed line.

Rectangle A runs the full width of the room, 14.5 ft. Its height is the left wall minus the notch height: 12 ft − 4.5 ft = 7.5 ft. Rectangle B is the part below the dashed line: the bottom edge, 9.25 ft, by the notch height, 4.5 ft.

A = 14.5 ft × 7.5 ft = 108.75 sq ft B = 9.25 ft × 4.5 ft = 41.625 sq ft Floor area = 108.75 + 41.625 = 150.375 sq ft

A quick estimate checks the size of the answer. The whole box around the L is 14.5 ft × 12 ft = 174 sq ft, and the notch takes a small corner out of it, so an answer near 150 sq ft is reasonable. An answer like 1,500 or 15 would point to a slipped decimal.

Task 3 (about 5 minutes, calculator)

Find the floor area two other ways and show that both give 150.375 sq ft.

  1. Cut the L with a vertical line instead, straight down from the top wall along the notch's left edge. Give the length, width and area of each rectangle.
  2. Take the whole 14.5 ft × 12 ft box and subtract the notch, 5.25 ft × 4.5 ft.
Solution to Task 3
  1. The vertical line is 9.25 ft from the left wall. The left rectangle is 9.25 ft wide and the full 12 ft tall: 9.25 × 12 = 111 sq ft. The right rectangle is 5.25 ft wide and 7.5 ft tall: 5.25 × 7.5 = 39.375 sq ft. Total: 111 + 39.375 = 150.375 sq ft.
  2. Box: 14.5 × 12 = 174 sq ft. Notch: 5.25 × 4.5 = 23.625 sq ft. Floor: 174 − 23.625 = 150.375 sq ft.

All three ways agree because each counts every square foot of the floor exactly once. When two methods agree, an arithmetic slip in either one is unlikely.

4. Square feet, square yards, and area on paper

One yard is 3 feet. A square yard is a square 1 yd on each side, so it is 3 ft by 3 ft, and it holds 3 × 3 = 9 square feet. The conversion factor for area is the length factor squared.

150.375 sq ft × (1 sq yd / 9 sq ft) = 16.71 sq yd (rounded)

Dividing by 3 instead of 9 gives 50.1 "square yards", three times too much. Prices convert with the same factor 9: a price per square yard is 9 times the price per square foot, because one square yard holds 9 square feet.

The same squaring happens between the paper and the room. One inch on paper stands for 4 ft, so one square inch on paper stands for a 4 ft by 4 ft square of floor:

1 sq in on paper = 4 ft × 4 ft = 16 sq ft of floor

Task 4 (about 4 minutes, calculator)

  1. Find the area of the L on paper, in square inches, from the paper lengths in Figure 1: the box 3 5/8 in × 3 in minus the notch 1 5/16 in × 1 1/8 in. Use decimals.
  2. Convert it to square feet of floor. You should get 150.375 sq ft again.
  3. What answer would you get by multiplying by 4 instead of 16?
  4. Write the flooring price, $2.49 per sq ft [1], as a price per square yard. Then check that 16.71 sq yd times that price gives the same cost as 150.375 sq ft × $2.49 per sq ft, to the nearest dollar.
Solution to Task 4
  1. Box: 3.625 × 3 = 10.875 sq in. Notch: 1.3125 × 1.125 = 1.4765625 sq in. Paper area: 10.875 − 1.4765625 = 9.3984375 sq in.
  2. 9.3984375 sq in × (16 sq ft / 1 sq in) = 150.375 sq ft.
  3. 9.3984375 × 4 = 37.59 sq ft (rounded), a quarter of the true area. Rectangle A alone is 108.75 sq ft, so a floor of 38 sq ft is impossible, and an estimate catches this mistake.
  4. $2.49 per sq ft × (9 sq ft / 1 sq yd) = $22.41 per sq yd. Then 16.71 sq yd × $22.41 per sq yd = $374.47, and 150.375 sq ft × $2.49 per sq ft = $374.43. Both are $374 to the nearest dollar; the 4 cents come from rounding 16.708 to 16.71. With the unrounded 16.708333 sq yd the two costs agree exactly.

5. Area to cover, waste allowance and whole cases

The new floor goes around the base cabinets, not under them. The installation instructions for this flooring say: "Kitchen cabinets must be installed before the floor is installed. Do not install cabinets on top of LifeProof Vinyl Plank." [4] So the area to cover is the floor area minus the cabinet footprint. From Task 1, the cabinet run is 11.5 ft long and 2 ft deep.

Cabinets = 11.5 ft × 2 ft = 23 sq ft Area to cover = 150.375 − 23 = 127.375 sq ft

Planks are cut to fit at walls and corners, and some cut pieces are too short to use. So you buy more flooring than the area to cover. The Home Depot installation guide for this flooring line says: "Allow 10 percent additional flooring for matching and waste." [2]

Adding 10% is multiplying by 1 + 0.10 = 1.10, as with percentages as multipliers in week 4:

127.375 sq ft × 1.10 = 140.1125 sq ft to buy

The store sells whole cases of 20.06 sq ft [1]. The number of cases is the area to buy divided by the area per case. The answer will not usually be a whole number, and you cannot buy part of a case, so round up to the next whole number. Rounding down would leave part of the floor bare.

Task 5 (about 4 minutes, calculator)

  1. How many cases of 20.06 sq ft cover 140.1125 sq ft? Give the exact quotient and the whole number of cases to buy.
  2. What do those cases cost at $49.95 a case [1], before sales tax?
  3. Compare with 127.375 sq ft × $2.49 per sq ft. Which is the real bill, and why are they different?
  4. Suppose you forgot to subtract the cabinets and ordered for the whole 150.375 sq ft, plus 10%. How many cases would you buy, and how much more would you spend?
Solution to Task 5
  1. 140.1125 ÷ 20.06 = 6.985 cases (rounded). Six cases cover 6 × 20.06 = 120.36 sq ft, which is less than 140.1125, so buy 7 cases. They cover 7 × 20.06 = 140.42 sq ft, just enough.
  2. 7 × $49.95 = $349.65 before sales tax.
  3. 127.375 × $2.49 = $317.16 (rounded). That is the price of the bare area to cover, with no waste and no part cases. The real bill is $349.65: 10% more flooring for waste, then rounded up to whole cases. The price per square foot and the price per case agree: $49.95 ÷ 20.06 sq ft = $2.49 per sq ft (rounded).
  4. 150.375 × 1.10 = 165.4125 sq ft, and 165.4125 ÷ 20.06 = 8.246 (rounded), so 9 cases for 9 × $49.95 = $449.55. That is 2 cases and $99.90 more than needed. The cabinet strip is 23 sq ft, about 15% of the floor, and it pushes the order past two case boundaries.

Estimate check: about 127 sq ft at about $2.50 per sq ft is about $320 for the bare area, and the bill should be roughly 10% above that because of the waste allowance. $349.65 fits.

The $349.65 covers the flooring only. Trim, tools, sales tax and labor are not included, and the price is one store's online price on one date [1].

6. A spreadsheet for the flooring order

Each step so far depends on the one before: the lengths give the areas, the areas give the area to cover, the waste allowance gives the area to buy, and that gives the cases and the cost. A spreadsheet keeps the chain. Change one input, such as the waste percentage, and every later cell updates.

One new function is needed. ROUNDUP(number, 0) rounds a number up to the next whole number; the 0 means "no decimal places". For example ROUNDUP(6.985, 0) gives 7, and ROUNDUP(7, 0) stays 7.
