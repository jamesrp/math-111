Module: Buying a house (week 4)   Lecture: 3 of 3   Title: Saving for the down payment

Situation: A 20% down payment on a $845,000 home is $169,000. The question students will care about: how long does it take to save that, how much does interest on savings help, and how much can a monthly budget borrow if home prices keep rising?

Mathematics: Compound interest as repeated multiplication by a growth factor (connecting to 4.1's balance rule); the function A(t) = A₀(1 + r)ᵗ as an input-output rule and its graph; comparing linear growth (saving the same amount each month with no interest, slope from week 2) with exponential growth on one graph; a savings plan with monthly deposits as a geometric sum (reusing 4.2's formula); finding how many months a goal takes with a table or spreadsheet (no logarithms); running the 4.2 payment formula backwards to find the loan a given monthly payment supports; a brief look at home price growth as another exponential.

Learning objectives addressed: functions; exponents; graphing; linear versus exponential growth; basic algebra; basic Excel use; reading graphs.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Lecture 4.1 (What will the monthly payment be?): a King County median sale price of $845,000 (August 2026, NWMLS) with 20% down gives a loan of $676,000; the 30-year fixed rate was 7.28% on October 1, 2026 (Freddie Mac PMMS), so the monthly rate is 0.0728/12; the balance rule new balance = old balance × (1 + monthly rate) − payment, done by hand for a small made-up loan and then in a spreadsheet over 360 rows using a fixed cell reference ($B$2) for the rate and payment; finding the payment that brings the balance to zero by trial and error and bracketing: $4,625.28; early payments are mostly interest; total interest. Lecture 4.1 ended by writing f = 1 + 0.0728/12 and showing that after 360 months the payment is multiplied by the sum 1 + f + f² + … + f³⁵⁹, and asked for a formula that would give $4,625.28 in one step. Lecture 4.2: summation notation; geometric series and their sum (fⁿ − 1)/(f − 1); the payment formula P = L·r·fⁿ/(fⁿ − 1), checked against $4,625.28.
