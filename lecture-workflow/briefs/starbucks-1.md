Module: Ordering at Starbucks (week 6)   Lecture: 1 of 3   Title: Counting drink orders

Situation: A Starbucks menu offers a handful of drinks, several sizes, several milks and many syrups. The question students will care about: how many different drinks can you actually order, and how do you count them without listing them all?

Mathematics: Listing outcomes systematically; tree diagrams; the multiplication principle, with the condition that each choice has the same number of options regardless of earlier choices; the addition principle for choices that exclude each other (this drink or that drink); counting when some combinations are not offered (subtracting the ones that are not allowed); orders of magnitude for large counts.

Learning objectives addressed: counting; arithmetic; reading tables; word problems.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Week 4 (Buying a house): percentages as multipliers; a balance rule in a spreadsheet with fixed cell references; summation notation; geometric sums; the mortgage payment formula; compound interest and exponential growth; linear versus exponential growth. Week 5: scale drawings, area, the Pythagorean theorem, square roots, volume, surface area, scaling.

Data and sources (the writer must find, verify and cite each; save the source text in draft/src/):
- The real current Starbucks menu options used: hot drink sizes (Short, Tall, Grande, Venti and their fluid ounces), milk choices, and a set of syrups, from starbucks.com (menu or nutrition pages) with the access date. Quote what the source lists. Where the menu varies by store or season, say so.

In-class tools: calculators (phones are fine), laptops with Excel or Google Sheets, projector, whiteboard.

Format and length: a single self-contained HTML page (inline CSS and JavaScript, no external dependencies, no MathJax; math in plain HTML) that the instructor projects and students read afterwards, and that works on a phone. Header line "Math 111 · Module: Ordering at Starbucks (week 6) · Lecture 1 of 3", then the lecture title. Numbered sections with plain titles that name their content. In-class tasks are boxed and numbered with time and tool in the label, for example "Task 2 (about 4 minutes, calculator)", each saying exactly what to produce, with its solution in a collapsed box right after it; task times add up to about half the period. A Sources list at the end, cited in the text with bracketed numbers, each source with its date and the figure quoted. Include an interactive element (for example a slider or a draggable point that redraws a graph) only if students use it in a task; when the lecture has graphs, draw them with inline SVG or canvas. Where students use a spreadsheet, give the layout as a CSV download and a copy-to-clipboard layout, with formulas, and say they were not tested in Excel or Google Sheets unless you tested them. The lecture period is 50 minutes, including in-class tasks.
