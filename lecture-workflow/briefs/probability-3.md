Module: Probability and the stock market (week 7)   Lecture: 3 of 3   Title: Expected return and risk

Situation: An investor hears that the stock market averages about 10% a year. The question students will care about: what does an average return mean for one person's money over a few years, and why can a 50% gain followed by a 50% loss leave you down 25%?

Mathematics: Expected value as a probability-weighted average, first with a simple game, then with yearly returns; gains and losses as positive and negative numbers; returns as multipliers (1 + r), so consecutive returns multiply rather than add; arithmetic mean versus the growth rate actually achieved (geometric mean computed as a root on a calculator or spreadsheet), with the +50% / −50% example; spread of outcomes as risk, shown with real annual returns; expected value is not a guarantee.

Learning objectives addressed: probability; expected value; negative numbers; percentages; roots on a calculator; basic Excel use; reading tables and graphs.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Week 4 (Buying a house): percentages as multipliers; a balance rule in a spreadsheet with fixed cell references; summation notation; geometric sums; the mortgage payment formula; compound interest and exponential growth; linear versus exponential growth. Week 5: scale drawings, area, the Pythagorean theorem, square roots, volume, surface area, scaling. Week 6: the multiplication principle, sets, subsets, Venn diagrams, permutations and combinations. Lecture 7.1: probability by counting. Lecture 7.2: estimating probabilities from data; independence; simulating up and down days and random walks in a spreadsheet.
