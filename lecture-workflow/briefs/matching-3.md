Module: Stable matching (week 9)   Lecture: 3 of 3   Title: Why the algorithm works

Situation: The algorithm from lecture 9.2 always gives a stable matching, but it treats the two sides differently. The question students will care about: how do mathematicians make sure a procedure always works, and who gets the better deal, the side that proposes or the side that responds?

Mathematics: Statements, 'if-then' statements, and their converse, with everyday and mathematical examples; proof by contradiction, introduced on the stability of the algorithm's output: suppose there is a blocking pair, follow the rounds, reach a contradiction; the proposing side gets its best possible stable partner (stated, illustrated by running the algorithm both ways on one example, proof sketched or omitted); real uses in school choice (for example New York City or Boston high school admissions) and kidney exchange.

Learning objectives addressed: logic; reading and constructing a simple argument; reading tables.

Students already know: Lectures 9.1 and 9.2: stable matchings, blocking pairs, the deferred acceptance algorithm and why it stops. From earlier weeks only arithmetic and n!.
