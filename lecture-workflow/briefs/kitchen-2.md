Module: Remodeling a kitchen (week 5)   Lecture: 2 of 3   Title: Square roots and the Pythagorean theorem

Situation: A new refrigerator has to get through a doorway, and a tall cabinet has to be tipped upright inside a room with a ceiling. The question students will care about: will it fit, and how do carpenters check that a corner is square with only a tape measure?

Mathematics: Squares and square roots, with calculator use and estimation between perfect squares; the Pythagorean theorem with a short visual reason (rearranging squares) and its converse; the 3-4-5 rule (and 6-8-10) for checking a square corner; the diagonal of a rectangle (a box tipped on its edge sweeps out its diagonal); deciding whether an object fits by comparing lengths; rounding up safely when the answer is a clearance.

Learning objectives addressed: squares and square roots; calculator use; order of operations; reading drawings; word problems.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Week 4 (Buying a house): percentages as multipliers; a balance rule in a spreadsheet with fixed cell references; summation notation; geometric sums; the mortgage payment formula; compound interest and exponential growth; linear versus exponential growth. Lecture 5.1: scale drawings; feet, inches and fractions of an inch; area of rectangles and L-shapes; area unit conversion; waste allowance; cost.

Data and sources (the writer must find, verify and cite each; save the source text in draft/src/):
- The dimensions of one real refrigerator model from the manufacturer's specification sheet (height, width, depth, and depth without doors if listed), cited with model number.
- A standard interior door width or clear opening (for example from the International Residential Code or the ADA Standards' 32-inch clear width), quoted.
- A standard ceiling height (for example the IRC minimum or the common 8 ft), quoted if used.
- The 3-4-5 method as described in a carpentry or building source, if quoted.

In-class tools: calculators (phones are fine), laptops with Excel or Google Sheets, projector, whiteboard.

Format and length: a single self-contained HTML page (inline CSS and JavaScript, no external dependencies, no MathJax; math in plain HTML) that the instructor projects and students read afterwards, and that works on a phone. Header line "Math 111 · Module: Remodeling a kitchen (week 5) · Lecture 2 of 3", then the lecture title. Numbered sections with plain titles that name their content. In-class tasks are boxed and numbered with time and tool in the label, for example "Task 2 (about 4 minutes, calculator)", each saying exactly what to produce, with its solution in a collapsed box right after it; task times add up to about half the period. A Sources list at the end, cited in the text with bracketed numbers, each source with its date and the figure quoted. Include an interactive element (for example a slider or a draggable point that redraws a graph) only if students use it in a task; when the lecture has graphs, draw them with inline SVG or canvas. Where students use a spreadsheet, give the layout as a CSV download and a copy-to-clipboard layout, with formulas, and say they were not tested in Excel or Google Sheets unless you tested them. The lecture period is 50 minutes, including in-class tasks.

Note on network access: direct curl/wget to outside sites is blocked in this environment; use the WebFetch tool to read sources, and save the relevant source text in draft/src/. A number that stands for the world is real and sourced, or labeled as made up.
