Module: Statistics and regression (week 8)   Lecture: 2 of 3   Title: Scatterplots and regression lines

Situation: Two measurements on the same people or places (for example hours of sleep and a test score, or a country's income and life expectancy). The question students will care about: do they move together, what line best describes the relationship, and how far can you trust a prediction from it?

Mathematics: Scatterplots; positive and negative association; the correlation coefficient as a measure of how close points are to a line, read rather than derived; residuals; the least-squares regression line chosen to make the sum of squared residuals as small as possible (shown with an interactive line students adjust, if a task uses it); fitting it with SLOPE, INTERCEPT and a chart trendline in a spreadsheet; reading the slope with units as the change in y per unit of x (week 2); predicting and the danger of extrapolating beyond the data.

Learning objectives addressed: coordinate pairs; graphing lines; slope; summary statistics; basic Excel use; reading graphs; interpreting analyses.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Week 4 (Buying a house): percentages as multipliers; a balance rule in a spreadsheet with fixed cell references; summation notation; geometric sums; the mortgage payment formula; compound interest and exponential growth; linear versus exponential growth. Week 5: scale drawings, area, the Pythagorean theorem, square roots, volume, surface area, scaling. Week 6: the multiplication principle, sets, subsets, Venn diagrams, permutations and combinations. Week 7: probability by counting, independence, simulation in a spreadsheet, expected value, returns as multipliers. Lecture 8.1: mean, median, standard deviation, histograms, using a spreadsheet's AVERAGE, MEDIAN and STDEV.
