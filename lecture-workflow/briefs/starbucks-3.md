Module: Ordering at Starbucks (week 6)   Lecture: 3 of 3   Title: Permutations and combinations

Situation: A group of friends orders a round of drinks, and someone has to choose which seasonal drinks to try or in what order to pick them up. The question students will care about: when does order matter, and how do you count choices of 3 from 10 without listing them?

Mathematics: Arrangements of n distinct items, n!, from the multiplication principle; permutations, choosing and ordering k of n; combinations, choosing k of n when order does not matter, as permutations divided by k! (each unordered choice is counted k! times), with a small listed example first; deciding whether order matters from the wording; computing with a calculator and with spreadsheet functions FACT, PERMUT and COMBIN; connecting C(n, k) summed over k to the 2ⁿ subsets from lecture 6.2.

Learning objectives addressed: counting; arithmetic; calculator use; basic Excel use; word problems.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Week 4 (Buying a house): percentages as multipliers; a balance rule in a spreadsheet with fixed cell references; summation notation; geometric sums; the mortgage payment formula; compound interest and exponential growth; linear versus exponential growth. Week 5: scale drawings, area, the Pythagorean theorem, square roots, volume, surface area, scaling. Lecture 6.1: tree diagrams, multiplication and addition principles. Lecture 6.2: sets, subsets and the 2ⁿ count, union, intersection, complement, Venn diagrams, inclusion-exclusion.
