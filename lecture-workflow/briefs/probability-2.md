Module: Probability and the stock market (week 7)   Lecture: 2 of 3   Title: Independence and simulation

Situation: The S&P 500 goes up on some days and down on others. The question students will care about: is a day's move like a coin flip, what is the chance of five up days in a row, and what does a simulated year of coin-flip days look like compared with the real index?

Mathematics: Estimating a probability from data (the fraction of up days in a real period); independent events and the multiplication rule P(A and B) = P(A)P(B), with an example where events are not independent; the chance of a streak; simulating random days in a spreadsheet with RAND() and IF, then a random walk of the index by applying a daily percent change (percent multipliers from week 4); comparing many simulated paths with the real path; one run versus the long run. A simulation the instructor can rerun on screen is welcome if a task uses it.

Learning objectives addressed: probability calculations; percentages as multipliers; basic Excel use (formulas, copying down, RAND); reading graphs.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Week 4 (Buying a house): percentages as multipliers; a balance rule in a spreadsheet with fixed cell references; summation notation; geometric sums; the mortgage payment formula; compound interest and exponential growth; linear versus exponential growth. Week 5: scale drawings, area, the Pythagorean theorem, square roots, volume, surface area, scaling. Week 6: the multiplication principle, sets, subsets, Venn diagrams, permutations and combinations. Lecture 7.1: sample spaces and events; equally likely outcomes; complements; P(A or B); counting favorable outcomes; probability as long-run frequency.
