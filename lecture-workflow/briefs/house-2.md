Module: Buying a house (week 4)   Lecture: 2 of 3   Title: A formula for the monthly payment

Situation: Lecture 4.1 found the payment on a $676,000, 30-year loan at 7.28% by trial and error in a spreadsheet: $4,625.28. It ended with the sum 1 + f + f² + … + f³⁵⁹. The question students will care about: is there one calculation that gives the payment for any loan, rate and length, and does it agree with the spreadsheet?

Mathematics: Summation notation (Σ) introduced at the point it saves writing, with small examples; geometric sequences and the ratio between terms; the sum of a geometric series derived by the multiply-and-subtract trick on a small case first (for example 1 + 2 + 4 + … + 2⁹) and then in general, S = (fⁿ − 1)/(f − 1); setting the balance after n months to zero and solving for the payment P = L·r·fⁿ/(fⁿ − 1) with r = monthly rate and f = 1 + r; checking it gives $4,625.28 to the cent; using it in one spreadsheet cell; then using it to answer what-if questions quickly (the 15-year loan at 6.60%; a different price or down payment).

Learning objectives addressed: summation notation; exponents; basic algebra (solving an equation for one quantity); calculator use; order of operations; basic Excel use.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Lecture 4.1 (What will the monthly payment be?): a King County median sale price of $845,000 (August 2026, NWMLS) with 20% down gives a loan of $676,000; the 30-year fixed rate was 7.28% on October 1, 2026 (Freddie Mac PMMS), so the monthly rate is 0.0728/12; the balance rule new balance = old balance × (1 + monthly rate) − payment, done by hand for a small made-up loan and then in a spreadsheet over 360 rows using a fixed cell reference ($B$2) for the rate and payment; finding the payment that brings the balance to zero by trial and error and bracketing: $4,625.28; early payments are mostly interest; total interest. Lecture 4.1 ended by writing f = 1 + 0.0728/12 and showing that after 360 months the payment is multiplied by the sum 1 + f + f² + … + f³⁵⁹, and asked for a formula that would give $4,625.28 in one step.
