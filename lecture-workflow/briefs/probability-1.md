Module: Probability and the stock market (week 7)   Lecture: 1 of 3   Title: Probability by counting

Situation: Before stocks, start with chance that can be counted: dice, cards, or a random drink from the Starbucks menu of week 6. The question students will care about: what does a probability of 1/6 or 30% actually mean, and how do you compute one when the outcomes are too many to list?

Mathematics: Sample space and events as sets (week 6 vocabulary); probability as favorable over total when outcomes are equally likely, and why the condition matters; probabilities as fractions, decimals and percentages; the complement rule P(not A) = 1 − P(A); P(A or B) by inclusion-exclusion; using permutations and combinations to count favorable outcomes (for example the chance a random 3-drink order contains a given drink); long-run frequency as the meaning of probability, shown with a quick class or spreadsheet experiment.

Learning objectives addressed: introductory probability calculations; set theory; fractions, decimals and percentages; counting; word problems.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Week 4 (Buying a house): percentages as multipliers; a balance rule in a spreadsheet with fixed cell references; summation notation; geometric sums; the mortgage payment formula; compound interest and exponential growth; linear versus exponential growth. Week 5: scale drawings, area, the Pythagorean theorem, square roots, volume, surface area, scaling. Week 6: the multiplication principle, sets, subsets, Venn diagrams, permutations and combinations.
