Module: Breakfast on a budget (week 3)   Lecture: 3 of 3   Title: Minimizing cost

Situation: Among all breakfasts that meet the requirements from lecture 3.2, which one is cheapest? The question students will care about: how do you find the cheapest good breakfast without checking every possibility, and what happens to the answer when a price changes?

Mathematics: The cost as a linear function of the two amounts; level lines of cost (all breakfasts costing the same amount) and sliding them across the feasible region; the corner-point method: the minimum of a linear cost over a polygon occurs at a corner; computing cost at each corner; how a price change can move the answer to a different corner; checking the answer with a spreadsheet solver (Excel Solver or the Google Sheets Solver add-on) described step by step, with what to do if Solver is not available. An interactive graph with a slider for a price that moves the cost line is welcome if a task uses it.

Learning objectives addressed: linear functions; inequalities; systems of equations; basic Excel use; reading graphs; word problems.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Lecture 3.1: inequalities and half-planes. Lecture 3.2: systems of inequalities, the feasible region, corner points by solving pairs of boundary equations, empty regions.

Data and sources (the writer must find, verify and cite each; save the source text in draft/src/):
- The same foods, nutrition values and prices as lectures 3.1 and 3.2 (cited); if prices are updated, use the newest month of the same BLS series and say so.

In-class tools: calculators (phones are fine), laptops with Excel or Google Sheets, projector, whiteboard.
