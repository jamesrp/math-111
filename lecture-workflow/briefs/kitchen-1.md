Module: Remodeling a kitchen (week 5)   Lecture: 1 of 3   Title: Reading a shop drawing

Situation: A kitchen remodel starts from a floor plan drawn to scale (for example 1/4 inch = 1 foot). The question students will care about: how big is the kitchen really, how much flooring do you need to buy, and what will it cost?

Mathematics: Scale factors as rates (inches on paper per foot in the room), using the unit conversion from week 1; feet and inches, and fractions of an inch (1/8, 1/16) as on a tape measure, adding and converting them; area of rectangles and of an L-shaped floor by splitting it; square feet versus square yards (area conversion factors are squared); waste allowance as a percentage; total cost from a price per square foot.

Learning objectives addressed: fractions; unit conversions; percentages; arithmetic; reading drawings and tables; word problems.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Week 4 (Buying a house): percentages as multipliers; a balance rule in a spreadsheet with fixed cell references; summation notation; geometric sums; the mortgage payment formula; compound interest and exponential growth; linear versus exponential growth.

Data and sources (the writer must find, verify and cite each; save the source text in draft/src/):
- A real, dated price per square foot for one flooring product from one retailer, labeled as that store's price on that date.
- The common flooring waste allowance (for example 10%) from a manufacturer's or retailer's installation guide, quoted.
- Standard kitchen cabinet dimensions (base cabinet depth 24 in, height 34.5 in, etc.) from a manufacturer specification or a published design guideline, if used.
- The floor plan itself is made up and labeled as made up.

In-class tools: calculators (phones are fine), laptops with Excel or Google Sheets, projector, whiteboard.

Format and length: a single self-contained HTML page (inline CSS and JavaScript, no external dependencies, no MathJax; math in plain HTML) that the instructor projects and students read afterwards, and that works on a phone. Header line "Math 111 · Module: Remodeling a kitchen (week 5) · Lecture 1 of 3", then the lecture title. Numbered sections with plain titles that name their content. In-class tasks are boxed and numbered with time and tool in the label, for example "Task 2 (about 4 minutes, calculator)", each saying exactly what to produce, with its solution in a collapsed box right after it; task times add up to about half the period. A Sources list at the end, cited in the text with bracketed numbers, each source with its date and the figure quoted. Include an interactive element (for example a slider or a draggable point that redraws a graph) only if students use it in a task; when the lecture has graphs, draw them with inline SVG or canvas. Where students use a spreadsheet, give the layout as a CSV download and a copy-to-clipboard layout, with formulas, and say they were not tested in Excel or Google Sheets unless you tested them. The lecture period is 50 minutes, including in-class tasks.

Note on network access: direct curl/wget to outside sites is blocked in this environment; use the WebFetch tool to read sources, and save the relevant source text in draft/src/. A number that stands for the world is real and sourced, or labeled as made up.
