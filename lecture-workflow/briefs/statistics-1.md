Module: Statistics and regression (week 8)   Lecture: 1 of 3   Title: Mean, median and standard deviation

Situation: Psychology is the largest group of students in this course, and psychology research reports results with means and standard deviations. Use a real, public data set from a psychology study (for example reaction times, survey scores, or hours of sleep). The question students will care about: what single numbers describe the data, and what does 'mean 7.1 hours, SD 1.2' tell you about a typical person?

Mathematics: Mean with summation notation (from 4.2) and its sensitivity to extreme values; median and when it is the better summary; range; standard deviation as a typical distance from the mean, computed step by step for a small set (squares and a square root, from week 5) and then with AVERAGE, MEDIAN and STDEV in a spreadsheet; histograms and how to read them; roughly how much of a bell-shaped data set lies within one or two standard deviations, stated as a rule of thumb.

Learning objectives addressed: summation notation; squares and square roots; reading tables and graphs; basic Excel use; interpreting summary statistics.

Students already know: Week 1 (lectures 1.1 to 1.3): rates written as fractions and unit conversion by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1; order of operations on a calculator; estimating the size of an answer; converting between fractions, decimals and percentages; concentrations and dilution; Spreadsheet skills so far (lecture 1.3): columns with a header row, formulas that refer to other cells, copying a formula down with relative references. Fixed cell references ($A$1) are not taught until lecture 4.1; do not use them. Week 2 (lectures 2.1 to 2.3): linear equations from nutrition labels; solving equations in one unknown; coordinate pairs; graphing lines; intercepts; slope from two points as a trade-off rate; y = mx + b; solving two equations in two unknowns by graphing and substitution; parallel and identical lines. Week 3: inequalities, feasible regions, corner points, minimizing a linear cost. Week 4 (Buying a house): percentages as multipliers; a balance rule in a spreadsheet with fixed cell references; summation notation; geometric sums; the mortgage payment formula; compound interest and exponential growth; linear versus exponential growth. Week 5: scale drawings, area, the Pythagorean theorem, square roots, volume, surface area, scaling. Week 6: the multiplication principle, sets, subsets, Venn diagrams, permutations and combinations. Week 7: probability by counting, independence, simulation in a spreadsheet, expected value, returns as multipliers.
