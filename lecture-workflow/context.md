Math 111 at the University of Washington serves students outside STEM who need one college mathematics course. The largest groups are Psychology and Nursing majors; Construction and Architecture students also take it. Many students dislike mathematics or lack confidence in it.

The redesign aims to make that one course worthwhile. Students should leave with practical quantitative skills, with experience turning real situations into mathematical models, and with a glimpse of mathematics they might want to study further.

The course is organized into modules, each built around a familiar situation or life event (for example buying a house, planning meals on a budget, or ordering at a coffee shop). Each module has several lectures, homework, and quizzes. A lecture starts from a concrete situation that students can explore with numbers, then increases the complexity until mathematical structure becomes useful.

Students are expected to use calculators and spreadsheets (Excel). Departmental learning objectives include confident arithmetic with fractions, decimals, percentages, and negative numbers; graphing lines and computing slopes; basic algebra and functions; introductory sets and probability; reading tables and graphs; and basic spreadsheet use.
