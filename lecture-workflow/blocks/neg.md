Leave these out. The instructors delete them every time they appear.

- Headings and boxes that carry no content: "Learning objectives", "Key takeaways", "Fun fact", "Did you know?", "Real-world connection", "Pro tip", unless the brief asks for one.
- Clever or atmospheric section titles ("Hunting for the payment", "Where the money goes", "The question for next time"). Name a section plainly for its content: "Mortgages", "Using a spreadsheet", "Finding the right monthly payment".
- Encouragement and reassurance: "Don't worry!", "You've got this", "Math can be fun", "Great question", and exclamation marks. Reassurance belongs to the instructor in the room.
- Talk about the lecture itself: "In this lecture we will explore…", "Let's dive in", "Now that we've covered…".
- Writing tics: "not X but Y" or "it's not just X, it's Y" contrasts, lists of three for rhythm, dashes used for asides, "Imagine…" openings, and rhetorical questions answered in the next sentence.
- Sentences that narrate a figure or table ("The table below shows…", "As you can see in the graph…"), and closing summaries that repeat the section.
- Inflated significance: "this powerful technique", "one of the most important ideas in mathematics", "you will use this every day".
- Stacked hedges and disclaimers: "Note that…", "It is important to remember that…", "Keep in mind…", qualifications on every claim.
- Invented characters and stories that carry no mathematics ("Meet Maya, a nursing student who…"). The situation is enough.
- Vague or invented real-world numbers. A number that stands for the world is real and sourced, or it is labeled as made up for the example.
